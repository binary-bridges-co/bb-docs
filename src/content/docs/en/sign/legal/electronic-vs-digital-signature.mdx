In Colombia, *firma electrónica* (electronic signature) and *firma digital* (digital signature) are
two distinct legal figures with distinct effects. bb-sign provides the first. This page gathers the
legal texts that matter, states precisely what bb-sign is and is not, and makes clear who decides
whether the mechanism suits a given document.

## What Ley 527 de 1999 says

The Spanish text is the authoritative one; a short paraphrase follows each quotation.

### Article 7: the signature

> **Artículo 7. Firma.** Cuando cualquier norma exija la presencia de una firma o establezca ciertas
> consecuencias en ausencia de la misma, en relación con un mensaje de datos, se entenderá
> satisfecho dicho requerimiento si:
>
> a) Se ha utilizado un método que permita identificar al iniciador de un mensaje de datos y para
> indicar que el contenido cuenta con su aprobación;
>
> b) Que el método sea tanto confiable como apropiado para el propósito por el cual el mensaje fue
> generado o comunicado.
>
> Lo dispuesto en este artículo se aplicará tanto si el requisito establecido en cualquier norma
> constituye una obligación, como si las normas simplemente prevén consecuencias en el caso de que
> no exista una firma.

Two conditions: the method identifies the originator and shows their approval of the content, and
it is reliable and appropriate for the purpose. The law does not require a specific technology.

### Article 28: the digital signature

> **Artículo 28. Atributos jurídicos de una firma digital.** Cuando una firma digital haya sido fijada
> en un mensaje de datos se presume que el suscriptor de aquella tenía la intención de acreditar ese
> mensaje de datos y de ser vinculado con el contenido del mismo.
>
> **Parágrafo.** El uso de una firma digital tendrá la misma fuerza y efectos que el uso de una firma
> manuscrita, si aquélla incorpora los siguientes atributos:
>
> 1. Es única a la persona que la usa.
> 2. Es susceptible de ser verificada.
> 3. Está bajo el control exclusivo de la persona que la usa.
> 4. Está ligada a la información o mensaje, de tal manera que si éstos son cambiados, la firma
>    digital es invalidada.
> 5. Está conforme a las reglamentaciones adoptadas por el Gobierno Nacional.

A digital signature is a particular case: a cryptographic value bound to a certificate issued to the
signing person by a Digital Certification Entity. Its advantage is the **presumption** in article
28: when it is affixed, the subscriber is presumed to have intended to endorse the message and be
bound by its content, without that intent having to be proven. The paragraph lists the attributes
(unique to the person, verifiable, under their exclusive control, bound to the message so that any
change invalidates it, compliant with government regulation) that give it the force of a handwritten
signature.

### Decreto 2364 de 2012: the electronic signature

Decreto 2364 de 2012, compiled in Decreto 1074 de 2015, regulates article 7 of Ley 527 and defines
the electronic signature as a method (codes, passwords, biometric data, private cryptographic keys or
others) that identifies a person in relation to a data message, provided it is reliable and
appropriate for the purposes it is used for, considering all the circumstances of the case and any
agreement between the parties. The decree treats the signature as reliable when the signature
creation data corresponds exclusively to the signer and any later alteration of the message is
detectable. An electronic signature has the value of a handwritten one, but without the presumption
of article 28: whoever relies on it must be able to prove the method.

## What bb-sign is

bb-sign is an **electronic signature** mechanism. The method, as recorded in the evidence of every
envelope, is:

- A **personal link** sent to the signer's email. Only whoever controls that mailbox can open it.
- Optionally, a **one-time code** sent to the same email, which the signer must type before signing.
  The organization decides, envelope by envelope, whether to require it.
- A **drawn or typed mark** by the signer, stamped into the document next to their name, email and
  the date.
- **Hash binding**: every signature is bound to the SHA-256 fingerprint of the documents the signer
  saw. Any later alteration is detectable.
- An **audit trail** with date and time, IP address and browser of every action.

By design this meets the two conditions of article 7: it identifies the signer and shows their
approval with a method the organization can explain and prove. See
[Electronic signature and seal](/en/sign/concepts/signature-and-seal/) and
[Evidence and audit](/en/sign/concepts/evidence-and-audit/).

## What bb-sign is not

- **It is not a digital signature** in the terms of article 28. Signers do not use a personal
  certificate issued in their name and there is no legal presumption about their intent. If your
  document requires a digital signature, bb-sign is not the mechanism.
- **It is not a qualified signature** under any jurisdiction. There is no notion of a signer with a
  qualified certificate in bb-sign.

## What bb-sign does not claim

To avoid confusion with other vendors' documentation:

- It does not claim compliance with **eIDAS** (European Union) or any of its levels (simple,
  advanced, qualified).
- It does not claim compliance with **ESIGN** or **UETA** (United States).
- It does not claim an **ISO 27001** certification.
- It does not offer **qualified signatures** or personal certificates to signers.
- It does not embed timestamps from an external authority (TSA), PAdES or long-term validation
  (LTV).

## The organization's seal is not the signer's signature

When an envelope completes, bb-sign stamps a **seal** on each document with the organization's
certificate. That seal proves two things: that the document did not change after sealing and which
organization sealed it. It says nothing about the identity of each signer; that is proven by the
electronic-signature evidence described above. Until the organization configures a certificate of
its own, the seal uses a Binary Bridges testing certificate that validators show as untrusted. See
[Your own certificate](/en/sign/admin/own-certificate/).

## The decision belongs to each customer

The law asks for a method that is "reliable and appropriate for the purpose". What is appropriate
depends on the document, the risk and the parties. That is why the
[Terms and conditions](/en/sign/legal/terms/) say so in clause 1.2: each customer, through its legal
office, decides whether the mechanism is appropriate for the documents it sends for signature. This
documentation gives the facts for that decision; it does not make it.
