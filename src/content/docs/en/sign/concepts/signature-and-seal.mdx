A document signed with bb-sign carries two marks that should not be confused. The **signature** is
the signer's: the stroke they drew or the name they typed. The **seal** is applied by bb-sign on
behalf of the organization that sent the envelope, with a digital certificate, and it is what makes
any later alteration of the PDF detectable. This page explains what each one is, what it contains
and what it proves.

## The signer's signature

On the "Add Your Signature" screen, the signer chooses "Draw" (they trace their signature with a
finger or the mouse) or "Type" (they type their full name and the application renders it in a
handwriting typeface). In both cases, what bb-sign stores is an **image** of that signature, together
with the time, the IP address and the browser it was submitted from. A signature image made outside
the application cannot be uploaded.

That image is an electronic signature in the sense of Decreto 2364 de 2012: data attached to the
message that identifies the signer and expresses their intent. It is not, by itself, cryptographic
proof. The proof comes from the seal.

## The organization's seal

When the last signer signs, bb-sign processes every document in the envelope in two steps.

### Each signer's visible seal

First it stamps on the PDF one seal image per signer, at the position the document's marker
indicates or, without one, at the bottom-right corner of the last page (see
[Documents](/en/sign/concepts/documents/)). The image contains:

| Element | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Name and email | The signer's, as the sender entered them. |
| Date | The day they signed, in UTC. |
| Signature | The image they drew or typed. |
| Hash | A SHA-256 digest computed from the name, the email, the date, the envelope identifier and the certificate's fingerprint; part of it appears as text around the circular seal, and the seal's pattern is derived from it. |
| QR code | Links to bb-sign's public verification page, with the envelope identifier and the start of the hash. See [Verify a signed document](/en/sign/signing/verify-a-document/). |

### The certificate signature

Then bb-sign signs the whole PDF with the organization's certificate: a detached CMS/PKCS#7
signature of type <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.signatureSubFilter} />, with the
<Fact of={CERTIFICATE.signatureAlgorithm} /> algorithm. It is a standard PDF signature: Adobe
Acrobat and any compatible reader show it in their signature panel and warn if the document changed
after it was signed.

That signature has a visible field at the bottom-right corner of the last page, reading "Digitally
signed by" followed by the name of the sealing organization and the date. It is not a signer's
seal: it is the organization's, and there is only one per document.

<Aside type="note" title="What the seal proves">
  The seal proves that the signed document is the one bb-sign produced when the envelope completed
  and that it **has not changed since**: if someone edits the PDF, the certificate signature stops
  validating. It also fixes who sealed it (the organization, through its certificate) and when.
  What the seal does not prove on its own is the signer's identity; that rests on the evidence of
  the process (email, verification code, IP address, audit trail). See
  [Evidence and audit](/en/sign/concepts/evidence-and-audit/).
</Aside>

## The default certificate

If the organization has not configured its own certificate, bb-sign seals with Binary Bridges'
testing certificate, whose subject is <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.defaultSubject} />. The visible field
then reads "Digitally signed by" <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.defaultDisplayName} />.

That certificate is **self-signed**: no authority backs it, and Adobe Acrobat and other readers show
it as untrusted. The document is still intact and the seal still detects changes, but a third party
cannot simply trust it. bb-sign never blocks a sending because of this.

For the seal to carry your organization's name and for readers to accept it, an administrator must
upload a certificate issued in the organization's name. How to choose one, where to buy it and how
to upload it is in [Your own certificate](/en/sign/admin/own-certificate/).

## Current limitation: leaf certificate only

Today bb-sign embeds in the PDF only the organization's certificate
(<Fact of={CERTIFICATE.chainEmbedded} />), not the chain of the authority that issued it. In
practice, a reader that does not have that authority's root installed cannot build the chain and
shows the signature as unverified even though the certificate is valid. Embedding the full chain is
planned, but does not exist yet.

## Electronic signature, not digital signature

Although the seal uses a digital certificate, bb-sign provides **electronic signature** in the terms
of Decreto 2364 de 2012, not *firma digital* under article 28 of Ley 527 de 1999. The difference and
what it means for each organization are in
[Electronic and digital signature](/en/sign/legal/electronic-vs-digital-signature/).
