Everything in bb-sign belongs to an **organization**. Inside it, envelopes live in **workspaces**,
and what each person or credential can do with an envelope depends on their **role in that
workspace**. What they can do with the organization itself (invite users, create label keys,
configure the certificate) depends on their **organization role**. This page explains the two layers
and how they combine.

## The organization

An organization is the boundary of everything: its users, its workspaces, its envelopes, its
documents, its label keys, its API credentials, its webhooks and its certificate. Nothing in one
organization is visible from another. A user belongs to one organization only, and an API credential
always acts on behalf of the organization that created it.

Organizations are created by Binary Bridges (the *platform administrator*).

## Workspaces

A workspace is a container for envelopes inside the organization. It separates areas (Human
Resources, Purchasing, Legal) and decides who sees what: a person only sees the envelopes of the
workspaces they have access to.

Every organization has a workspace called **General**, created with it. The application shows it as
"General (everyone)" because every member of the organization can work in it without being granted
access. General cannot be renamed or deleted; the application says so: "The General workspace can't
be renamed or deleted."

Administrators create the other workspaces and decide who joins each one. A workspace can only be
deleted when it has no envelopes, archived ones included. The operational details are in
[Workspaces and members](/en/sign/admin/workspaces-and-members/).

## Workspace roles

In each workspace, a person or an API credential holds one of four roles. The application calls
them **Viewer**, **Auditor**, **Contributor** and **Manager**; the API and the tables in this
documentation use `viewer`, `auditor`, `contributor` and `manager`. Each role includes everything
the previous one can do:

| Role | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| `viewer` (Viewer) | See the list and the detail of envelopes, archived ones included. Does not download documents. |
| `auditor` (Auditor) | Download documents and read each envelope's audit trail. |
| `contributor` (Contributor) | Create envelopes, edit them before sending, upload documents, assign labels and send. |
| `manager` (Manager) | Cancel envelopes. |

**Contributor is implicit in General.** Every member of the organization is a Contributor in the
General workspace, always. That role is neither granted nor revoked; if an administrator tries to
assign it, the application answers "That role can't be granted. Choose Viewer, Auditor or Manager."
In the other workspaces, a member has no role until an administrator grants one.

## Organization roles

The organization role decides which parts of the application a person sees and what they can do
outside envelopes:

| Role | Name in the application | What it can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `org_member` | Org Member | Work with envelopes according to their workspace roles. Sees the Dashboard, Envelopes and Archived. Does not see Users, Labels, Storage or Settings. |
| `org_admin` | Org Admin | All of the above in **every** workspace, without belonging to any, plus invite users, create workspaces and grant roles, define label keys, manage API credentials and webhooks, upload the certificate and read the organization's audit trail. |
| `org_api` | API credential | A machine. It only lists workspaces and label keys; what it can do with envelopes is decided by its workspace roles. It can never manage credentials, webhooks, workspaces or members. |
| `platform_admin` | Platform Admin | Binary Bridges. Creates organizations, sets quotas and the seal's appearance. |

An administrator sees every workspace in the organization without being a member of any. If someone
tries to add one to a workspace, the application refuses: "Admins already see every workspace." The
full permission matrix, including what an API credential can never hold, is in
[Permission matrix](/en/sign/admin/permissions/).

## What a member cannot do

An Org Member with the Contributor role creates, edits, sends and downloads. There are two things
they do not do:

- **Cancel an envelope.** The "Cancel Envelope" button only works for a Manager of the workspace or
  an administrator. If you need to cancel and cannot, ask your administrator to do it or to grant
  you the Manager role in that workspace. See [Cancel an envelope](/en/sign/sending/cancel/).
- **Administer the organization.** Users, label keys, workspaces, credentials, webhooks and the
  certificate are administrator tasks.

## The "404, not 403" rule

bb-sign has two ways of saying no:

- If you try an organization action your role does not hold (for example, creating a label key as
  a member), the answer is `403` and the application says "You don't have permission to do this."
- If you try to open an envelope or a workspace you **do not have access to**, the answer is `404`,
  the same as if it did not exist. The application says "Envelope not found, or you don't have
  access to it." or "That workspace no longer exists, or you can't use it." Those envelopes do not
  appear in lists or counters either.

<Aside type="note" title="A 404 does not tell you whether the envelope exists">
  An envelope you cannot access and an envelope that does not exist answer exactly the same. This is
  deliberate: nobody can discover what is in someone else's workspace by trying identifiers. If
  someone shares a link with you and you see "Envelope not found", you most likely lack access to its
  workspace. Ask an administrator for that access; do not look for a mistake in the link.
</Aside>

## Membership cap

A person or an API credential can belong to at most

application answers "This person or API key already belongs to the maximum number of workspaces".
Administrators do not count: they see every workspace without belonging to any.
