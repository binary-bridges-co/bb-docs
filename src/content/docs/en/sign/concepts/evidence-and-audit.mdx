An envelope's evidence is everything bb-sign keeps about the process: who received what, who opened
it, who signed, from where and when. This page says exactly what is recorded, what is not recorded
yet, and what the public verification the seal's QR code points to checks (and does not check).

## The audit chain

Every audit event carries its own hash and the hash of the previous event of the same envelope. If
someone deleted or altered an event, the next one would no longer fit it: the chain lets you check
that nothing was inserted or removed afterwards. Audit events are not purged today.

In an envelope's detail, the "Audit Trail" section shows every event with its date, its actor (a
person, a signer or "System") and its hash. Only Auditors and Managers of the workspace see it: "The
audit trail is available to auditors and managers of this workspace." Administrators see it in every
workspace.

## Which events are recorded

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| `SIGNER_ELIGIBLE` | It becomes a signer's turn (on send, or when the previous group finishes in sequential signing). |
| `SIGNING_LINK_CLICKED` | The signer opens their link. |
| `SIGNING_VIEW_OPENED` | The signer opens the signing view. |
| `SIGNING_VIEW_CLOSED` | The signer closes or leaves the signing view. |
| `SIGNER_SIGNED` | The signer submits their signature. |
| `ENVELOPE_COMPLETED` | The last signer signs. |
| `SIGNED_COPY_ACCESSED` | A signer downloads their signed copy. |
| `EMAIL_SENT`, `EMAIL_DELIVERED`, `EMAIL_OPENED`, `EMAIL_BOUNCED` | An envelope email is sent, delivered, opened or bounces, as reported by the email provider. |

At organization level, not envelope level, bb-sign also records the creation and revocation of API
credentials, the creation, deletion and secret rotation of webhooks, and the creation, renaming and
deletion of workspaces, with role grants and revocations. An administrator reads those events in
[Audit trail](/en/sign/admin/audit-trail/).

## What is not recorded today

So that you do not look for what is not there: creating, sending, cancelling and expiring an
envelope **do not generate audit events** today. Those facts are kept on the envelope itself (its
status and its creation, sent and cancelled dates), and sending, cancellation and expiry do fire
webhooks, but they are not part of the audit chain. Verifying the one-time code is not recorded as
an event either: the envelope keeps whether the code was required, and the signer cannot sign
without verifying it, but there is no `OTP_VERIFIED` event.

## What is kept with each signature

When the signer submits their signature, bb-sign keeps the signature **image**, the exact **time**,
the **IP address** the request came from and the **browser** (the *user agent* string). That data
accompanies the `SIGNER_SIGNED` event and the signer's record.

## View tracking

Every time the signer opens the signing view, the application reports it and `SIGNING_VIEW_OPENED`
is recorded. So that a reload or a retry does not count as separate openings, reports arriving
within <Fact of={ENVELOPE.viewDedupSeconds} unit="seconds" /> of the previous one are treated as the
same opening. A later opening after that window is recorded again.

## The public verification

The QR code on every seal leads to a public bb-sign page with two parameters: the envelope
identifier and the first sixteen characters of the seal's hash. Anyone with the document can use it
without an account. See [Verify a signed document](/en/sign/signing/verify-a-document/).

### What it checks

bb-sign looks up the signer of that envelope whose hash starts with the received value and
**recomputes** the hash with the formula <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.sealHashFormula} />, using the
fingerprint of the organization's certificate that is **active today**. If the result matches the
stored hash, the answer is "valid" and shows the signer's name, their email and the signing date.

### What it does not check

<Aside type="caution" title="Verification does not read the PDF">
  The verification page does not receive the document or inspect its certificate signature. It
  checks that the seal's data matches what bb-sign has on record, not that the PDF in your hands is
  intact. For that, open the document in Adobe Acrobat or another reader and review its signature
  panel. See [Electronic signature and seal](/en/sign/concepts/signature-and-seal/).
</Aside>

Because the hash includes the certificate's fingerprint, **changing the organization's certificate
makes earlier seals verify as invalid** on that page, even though the documents have not changed. If
your organization changes its certificate, tell whoever will verify older documents to do it with
the reader's signature panel, not with the QR code.

## What does not exist yet

bb-sign does not generate an **evidence certificate** PDF (a downloadable report with the event
chain, IP addresses and signatures of an envelope). Evidence is consulted in the envelope's "Audit
Trail", inside the application; the public API does not expose it. If you need to keep the evidence
outside bb-sign, consult it and save it before the envelope is archived; see
[Scope and retention](/en/sign/legal/retention/).
