bb-sign records complete evidence for every envelope: who received what, who opened it, who signed,
from where and when. That evidence, protected by an audit chain, supports each signer's identity and
the integrity of the process, and when the envelope completes it is delivered in an
[evidence certificate](/en/sign/concepts/evidence-certificate/). This page explains what is recorded
and how it is checked.

## The audit chain

Every audit event belongs to a chain: the envelope's, the organization's or the platform's. Within its
chain, each event carries a sequence number with no gaps and a hash that covers its content, its
position and the hash of the previous event. The chain therefore lets you check that no event was
inserted, modified or removed afterwards: any alteration breaks the link with the next event.

Events are only ever added. No user or service can modify or delete an event once recorded, and
bb-sign verifies every chain each night, at <Fact of={AUDIT.nightlyVerifyTime} /> (time zone
<Fact of={AUDIT.nightlyVerifyTimeZone} />).

## An envelope's audit trail

In an envelope's detail, the **Audit Trail** section shows every event with its date, its actor and
its hash. The actor can be:

- **A user**, by name or email ("Unknown user" when they can no longer be identified).
- **An API credential**, by its label and with the **API** badge ("API credential" when it can no
  longer be identified).
- **A signer**.
- **System**, for bb-sign's automatic actions.
- **Actor not recorded**, on older events that did not keep who acted.

The trail is available to Auditors, Contributors and Managers of the workspace, and to administrators
in every workspace. Since every member is a Contributor in General, everyone sees the trail of that
workspace's envelopes. See
[Organizations, workspaces and roles](/en/sign/concepts/organizations-workspaces-roles/).

## Data kept with each signature

When a signer submits their signature, bb-sign keeps:

- the signature **image**;
- the exact **time**;
- the originating **IP address** and the **browser** (the *user agent* string);
- the **identification method**: `EMAIL_LINK` (the link sent by email) or `EMAIL_LINK_OTP` (the link
  and a one-time code);
- if a code was used, its channel, the masked destination and the time it was accepted. The code is
  never stored in plain text;
- the **fingerprint of the document set** signed, which binds the signature to those exact documents.

This data accompanies the `SIGNER_SIGNED` event, the signer's record and the evidence certificate.

## Opening tracking

Every time the signer opens the signing view, `SIGNING_VIEW_OPENED` is recorded. So that a reload or
a retry does not count as separate openings, reports arriving within
<Fact of={ENVELOPE.viewDedupSeconds} unit="seconds" /> of the previous one are treated as the same
opening. An opening after that interval is recorded as a new one.

## The public verification

bb-sign's public verification page offers two checks, without an account. See
[Verify a signed document](/en/sign/signing/verify-a-document/).

### The seal's QR code

The QR code on every seal leads to the public page with the envelope identifier and the first
sixteen characters of the seal's hash. bb-sign looks up the signer of that envelope whose hash starts
with the received value and **recomputes** the hash from the recorded data:

- Seals made since bb-sign <Fact of={VERIFY.qrUsesStoredFingerprintSince} /> (seal version 2) use
  the formula <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.sealHashFormulaV2} />, where each `<len>` is the length in bytes
  (UTF-8) of the field that follows it, with the fingerprint of the certificate that sealed, stored
  with the seal, and the fingerprint of the document set, <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.documentSetFormula} />,
  with one entry per document ordered by `documentId`, which bb-sign recomputes from the documents'
  recorded fingerprints.
- Earlier seals (version 1) use the formula <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.sealHashFormulaV1} />, with the
  fingerprint of the organization's **active** certificate.

If the result matches the recorded hash, the page confirms the seal is valid and shows the signer's
name, their email and the signing date.

Version 2 seals keep validating even if your organization changes its certificate, because they carry
the fingerprint of the certificate that sealed them. Version 1 seals depend on the active certificate:
if your organization changes its certificate, they stop validating on the public page, even though
the documents are intact. For those documents, use the reader's signature panel and let whoever will
verify them know.

<Aside type="caution" title="Envelopes in Archived">
  Today, the QR code of an envelope that has moved to **Archived**
  (<Fact of={ENVELOPE.archiveAfterDays} unit="days" /> after its creation) shows "Invalid Signature",
  even though the document is intact. For those documents, use **Check a file** or, if they were
  sealed before that check existed, the reader's signature panel. See
  [Verify a signed document](/en/sign/signing/verify-a-document/).
</Aside>

### Check a file

On the same page, **Check a file** takes a PDF, computes its SHA-256 fingerprint on the server and
looks it up among the documents **signed** by bb-sign, those of archived envelopes included. The file
is not stored. If the fingerprint matches, the page shows when the envelope completed, the signers
(with their email masked and the time they signed) and the fingerprint of the certificate that
sealed. An unsigned original, or a document changed after signing, does not match. Nor do documents
sealed before this check existed: for those, use the reader's signature panel.

Each file can weigh up to <Fact of={VERIFY.fileMaxMb} unit="MB" /> and is checked within
<Fact of={VERIFY.fileMaxSeconds} unit="seconds" />. Each connection can run up to
<Fact of={VERIFY.fileRatePerMinute} /> checks per minute.

<Aside type="caution" title="To verify the PDF's signature, use the reader's signature panel">
  Neither check validates the PDF's certificate signature. To confirm that the file's signature is
  valid, open it in Adobe Acrobat or another reader and review its signature panel. See
  [Electronic signature and seal](/en/sign/concepts/signature-and-seal/).
</Aside>

## Consult and keep the evidence

Each envelope's evidence is consulted in its **Audit Trail**, inside the application. When the
envelope completes, bb-sign also delivers it in the **evidence certificate**, a sealed PDF, together
with the audit trail frozen as JSON. They are downloaded by whoever can download the envelope's
documents; signers do not receive them. bb-sign keeps them for
<Fact of={EVIDENCE.retentionDays} unit="days" /> from issue. See
[Evidence certificate](/en/sign/concepts/evidence-certificate/).

Integrations can also verify an envelope's chain (result
{AUDIT.verificationStatuses.value.join(', ')}) and export its trail with
<Fact of={AUDIT.exportPath} />, in the <Fact of={AUDIT.exportFormat} /> format, with the `audit:read`
permission. To keep the evidence beyond bb-sign's retention period, download it beforehand and see
[Scope and retention](/en/sign/legal/retention/).
