Anyone who has the signed PDF can verify it without an account. The QR code on the seal confirms
the signature details, and the organization's certificate seal confirms that the file has not
changed.

## Scan the QR code

Scanning the QR code on the seal opens the **Signature Verification** page, which shows the result
and four details: **Signer**, **Email**, **Signed** and **Document** (the envelope identifier). If
the link is incomplete, the page shows "This link is missing its verification details. Scan the QR
code on the signed document again, or open the link exactly as it appears there."

## "Valid Signature" result

**Valid Signature** means that the details on the seal (signer, email, date and envelope) match
what bb-sign recorded at the time of signing. To check this, bb-sign recomputes the seal's
fingerprint, <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.sealHashFormula} />, from the recorded details and the
organization's active certificate, and compares it with the fingerprint printed on the seal.

## "Invalid Signature" result

**Invalid Signature** means the fingerprint does not match. Possible causes:

- a QR code or link that does not correspond to a recorded signature, such as a badly copied link;
- a certificate change in the organization after the signature: the fingerprint includes the
  certificate, so earlier seals stop matching even though the document is intact;
- no active certificate to compare against ("Certificate not available for verification").

In any of these cases, the organization that sent the document can confirm the signature from the
envelope's history.

## Check the PDF's integrity

<Aside type="caution" title="Use the certificate seal to check the file">
  The QR code verifies the details recorded in bb-sign. To confirm that the PDF file was not
  modified after signing, check the certificate seal.
</Aside>

When the envelope completes, bb-sign seals every PDF with the organization's certificate. Open the
file in Adobe Acrobat Reader and open the signature panel (the pen icon in the side bar). There
you can see:

- whether the file changed after it was sealed, which Acrobat shows next to the signature;
- whose name the certificate is in. An organization with its own certificate appears under its
  name. If the organization uses the default Binary Bridges testing certificate, Acrobat marks it
  as untrusted. See [Your own certificate](/en/sign/admin/own-certificate/).

The PDF embeds the end certificate without the full chain, so Acrobat may ask you to trust it
manually.

## If the service does not respond

If the page shows "We could not reach the verification service. This does not mean the signature
is invalid — please try again in a moment.", the service is temporarily unavailable. Scan the QR
code again later.
