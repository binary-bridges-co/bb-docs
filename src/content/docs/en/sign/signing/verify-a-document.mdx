Anyone holding the signed PDF can check the seal without an account. This page explains what that
verification checks and, above all, what it does not.

## Scan the QR code

Every seal carries a QR code. Scanning it opens bb-sign's "Signature Verification" page, which
queries the server and shows a result and four details: "Signer", "Email", "Signed" and
"Document" (the envelope identifier). If you open the link by hand and something is missing, you
see "This link is missing its verification details. Scan the QR code on the signed document again,
or open the link exactly as it appears there."

## What "Valid Signature" means

Under the result you read "This document has not been altered since it was signed." Concretely,
bb-sign recomputes the seal's fingerprint, <Fact of={CERTIFICATE.sealHashFormula} />, from the
signer details it has stored and the certificate **active today** in the organization, and
compares it with the one printed on the seal. If they match, the details on the seal (who, with
which email, on which day, on which envelope) are the ones bb-sign recorded and nobody has changed
them.

## What "Invalid Signature" can mean

The page says "The document does not match its signature. It may have been edited after signing,
or this may not be the original file." The possible causes:

- The QR or the link does not correspond to any recorded signature (a badly copied link, for
  example).
- The organization changed its certificate after the signature. The fingerprint includes the
  certificate, so earlier seals stop matching even though the document is intact.
- There is no active certificate to compare against ("Certificate not available for
  verification").

If in doubt, ask the organization to confirm the signature from the envelope's history.

## What this check does not do

<Aside type="caution" title="The QR does not examine the PDF file">
  The verification compares details stored in bb-sign, not the file in front of you. A PDF edited
  after signing, with its seal intact, would still show "Valid Signature" when you scan its QR. To
  know whether the file changed, look at the certificate seal.
</Aside>

## Check the certificate seal

When the envelope completes, bb-sign signs every PDF with the organization's certificate. Open the
file in Adobe Acrobat Reader and open the signature panel (the pen icon in the side bar). There
you see:

- whether the file changed after it was sealed: Acrobat warns next to the signature;
- whose name the certificate is in. By default bb-sign uses a Binary Bridges testing certificate,
  which Acrobat marks as untrusted; an organization with its own certificate appears under its
  name. See [Your own certificate](/en/sign/admin/own-certificate/).

Today the PDF embeds only the end certificate, not the full chain, so Acrobat may ask you to trust
it manually.

## If the service does not respond

You see "We could not reach the verification service. This does not mean the signature is invalid
— please try again in a moment." It is a temporary failure of the service, not of the document:
scan the QR again later.
