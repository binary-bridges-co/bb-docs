On the **Add your signature** screen you sign the document and, once everyone has signed, download
your sealed copy.

## Draw, type or upload an image

Choose how to sign with the **Draw**, **Type** or **Upload image** tabs. The signature appears on a
white box, exactly as it will look on the document.

- **Draw**: under **Draw your signature**, sign above the line in the box with your finger, a stylus
  or the mouse. **Clear**, above the box, empties it.
- **Type**: enter your name in **Type your full name**. Below it, "How it will be stamped" shows your
  name in a handwriting font.
- **Upload image**: use a photo or scan of the signature you made by hand. The next section
  explains how.

Then press **Submit signature**. If there is no signature yet, you see "Please draw your
signature.", "Please type your name." or "Choose an image of your signature."

### Upload an image of your signature

The **Upload a photo of your signature** box tells you which image works: "PNG or JPEG, up to
<Fact of={SIGNATURE_UPLOAD.maxSourceMb} unit="MB" />." Below it, the page gives three tips: sign on
plain white paper with a dark pen, take the photo in even light without shadows, and your photo stays
on your device.

1. Sign on white paper and take a photo of it or scan it.
2. Press **Choose an image** and pick the file. On a phone you can take the photo with the camera
   or pick it from the gallery.
3. Check "How it will be stamped": it shows your signature exactly as it will be stamped on the
   document, with the file's name and size. To use another image, press **Replace image**. To start
   over, press **Remove**.

Once you choose the image, **Remove light background** appears, on by default: "Makes the paper
behind your signature transparent.", so that only the stroke appears in the seal. If the preview
does not look right, turn it off and check the preview again.

If the file is larger than <Fact of={SIGNATURE_UPLOAD.resizeNoticeMb} unit="MB" />, the page
shows: "This image is larger than <Fact of={SIGNATURE_UPLOAD.resizeNoticeMb} unit="MB" />, so it
will be reduced in size. If you don't want that, reduce the file size yourself and upload it
again." It is only a notice: you can submit the signature as it is, or reduce the file yourself and
pick it again.

The image is prepared on your device. Only the prepared signature image reaches bb-sign, without the
location and device data that photos often carry. If the page shows a message when you pick the
image, see [If something goes wrong](/en/sign/signing/troubleshooting/).

## Information that is recorded

When you submit your signature, bb-sign records, next to your name and email:

- the image of your signature;
- the form of the signature that the signing page declared: drawn, typed or uploaded image;
- the date and time;
- the IP address you signed from;
- the browser you used;
- how you were identified: with the link in your email only, or with the link and the verification
  code, with the channel, the masked destination and the time the code was accepted;
- the SHA-256 fingerprint of the set of documents you signed, which binds your signature to those
  exact documents.

The evidence certificate shows the form of the signature as "Signature mark (as declared by the
signing client)", with the value "drawn on screen", "name typed on screen" or "image uploaded from
the device". It is what the signing page declared: bb-sign does not infer it from the image. If the
signature was recorded without that declaration, the certificate shows "not recorded".

This information is part of the envelope's evidence, which the organization that sent you the
document keeps. Your name, email and the date also appear on the document's seal.

<Aside type="caution" title="A submitted signature is final">
  Once the signature is recorded, the envelope moves to the next step. If you signed by mistake or
  the details were not yours, contact the person who sent you the document, who can cancel the
  envelope or send a new one.
</Aside>

## Confirmation screen

After submitting you see **Document Signed Successfully!** and, depending on the moment, one of these
states of your signed copy:

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Download Your Signed Document** | The sealed copy is ready. There is one button per document. |
| "Preparing your signed copy — this can take a few moments." | Everyone has signed and bb-sign is sealing the documents. The page refreshes automatically. |
| "Your signed copy is still being prepared. We have emailed you a link — you can download it from there once it is ready." | Sealing is taking longer. You can close the page and download the copy from the completion email. |
| No download button | Other people still need to sign. The copy will be available once everyone has signed. |

## Completion email

When everyone has signed, you receive the email `Signing complete: {title}` with the link to your
copy. It is sent when bb-sign finishes processing the envelope{EVIDENCE.usuallyIssuedWithinMinutes.value && ', usually a few minutes after the last signature'},
and it never waits more than <Fact of={EVIDENCE.degradedAfterHours} unit="hours" />. If your copy is
still being prepared, the email says so: "Your signed documents are being prepared; they will appear
at the same link." The link opens the confirmation screen with the message "This
envelope is complete. You can download your signed copy below." You can use it as often as you need
while the copy is available.

## Copy availability

Your signed copy can be downloaded from your link for
<Fact of={RETENTION_POLICY.retentionDays} unit="days" /> from the envelope's completion, even after the
signing period has ended and even once the envelope is archived. We recommend saving your copy as soon
as you download it.

If your copy were ever in archive storage, the page says so and lets you ask for it. Today bb-sign
does not move any copy to that storage, but if it happens, you see:

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Your signed copy is archived. Request it and we will email it to you when it is available." and **Request my copy** | Your copy is in archive storage. | Press **Request my copy**. |
| "Your signed copy is being restored. Request it and we will email it to you when it is available." | Someone else has already asked for the copy. | Press **Request my copy** to receive the email too. |
| "We will email you your copy when it is available, usually within a few hours." | Your request was recorded. | Wait for the email `Tu solicitud de restauración de documentos / Your document restore request` and open its link. The restored copy can be downloaded for <Fact of={RESTORE.days} unit="days" />{RESTORE.requestableAgainAfterWindow.value && <>; after that you can press <strong>Request my copy</strong> again</>}. |
| "We received many requests from this link or your connection. If you already asked, the email will arrive; if not, try again in a few minutes." | The limit of <Fact of={RATE_LIMITS.signerRestorePerLink} /> requests per link, or <Fact of={RATE_LIMITS.signerRestorePerIp} /> per connection, within <Fact of={RATE_LIMITS.signerRestoreWindowMinutes} unit="minutes" /> was reached. | If you already asked, wait for the email. If not, try again in a few minutes. |
| "We could not request your copy. Try again in a few minutes." | The request did not complete. | Try again in a few minutes. |

Once the retention period has ended, the page shows "Your signed copy is no longer available: its
<Fact of={RETENTION_POLICY.retentionDays} />-day retention period has ended." In that case, request
the document from the person who sent it: from then on, keeping it is the sending organization's
responsibility.

## What the seal contains

Every signed document carries a seal, in the position set by the sender or at the end of the last
page. The seal includes the header "Verified Signature" with the envelope identifier, your
signature, your name, your email, the signing date (year-month-day, in UTC), a QR code and a
circular pattern generated from the signature's fingerprint.

The PDF also carries the organization's certificate seal, with its certificate chain, visible in the
PDF reader's signature panel. The seal's fingerprint includes the fingerprint of the set of documents
you signed and that of the certificate that sealed it, so the seal still verifies even if the
organization changes its certificate later. To check a signature, see [Verify a signed document](/en/sign/signing/verify-a-document/).
The relationship between your signature and the certificate seal is explained in
[Electronic signature and seal](/en/sign/concepts/signature-and-seal/).
